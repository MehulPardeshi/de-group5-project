**Idea 1: Predictive Maintenance for Manufacturing Equipment**

**Context (business, environment, sports, public services...)**

Manufacturing and industrial production.
Factories rely on expensive machinery, including motors, pumps, conveyor belts, turbines, and robotic arms. These machines often operate continuously, and unexpected equipment failures can disrupt entire production lines.

**The real-world problem**

Unexpected machine breakdowns cause production delays, expensive repairs, wasted materials, and reduced productivity.
Many manufacturers still rely on scheduled maintenance or react only after equipment fails. This can result in unnecessary maintenance or failures that could have been prevented.
The goal is to build a data pipeline that collects machine sensor data, identifies abnormal behavior, and helps predict potential equipment failures before they happen.

**Who would benefit?**

    Manufacturing companies and factory owners.
    Maintenance engineers and technicians.
    Production managers responsible for minimizing downtime.
    Industrial automation companies.
    Supply chain managers affected by production delays.

**Any datasets you know of, with links**

[AI4I Predictive Maintenance Dataset 
](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/shivamb/machine-predictive-maintenance-classification)	
	
**Why you care about it**

I am interested in manufacturing and industrial automation, and I find it interesting how data can help make factories more efficient and reliable.
This project would allow me to explore how machine sensor data can be transformed into useful insights. 
It would also give me practical experience building an end-to-end data engineering pipeline while solving a problem that has a direct financial impact on manufacturing businesses.


**Idea 2: Quality Control and Defect Detection Using Robotics and Computer Vision**

**Context (business, environment, sports, public services...)**

Manufacturing, robotics, and industrial automation.
Modern factories use robotic arms, cameras, and computer vision systems to inspect products during manufacturing. Automated quality inspection helps companies identify defective products before they reach customers.

**The real-world problem**

Manufacturing companies need to ensure that products meet quality standards.
Traditional manual inspections can be slow, expensive, and inconsistent. Small defects such as scratches, cracks, dents, and incorrect assembly can go unnoticed.
The goal is to create a data engineering pipeline that processes product inspection images, stores inspection results, identifies defect patterns, and generates reports about product quality.
For example, a robotic arm equipped with a camera could inspect manufactured components and classify them as defective or defect-free.

**Who would benefit?**

    Manufacturing companies and production facilities.
    Robotics and industrial automation companies.
    Quality assurance engineers.
    Factory supervisors and production managers.
    Automotive, electronics, and aerospace manufacturers.

**Any datasets you know of, with links**

[Amazon Robotic Manipulation Benchmark (ARMBench) 
](https://www.amazon.science/code-and-datasets/amazon-robotic-manipulation-benchmark-armbench)

[NIST Robotics Datasets
](https://www.nist.gov/el/robotics)

**Why you care about it**

I am interested in robotics and how automated systems are transforming manufacturing.
I find it interesting how robots can work alongside computer vision and data analytics to improve product quality and reduce human error.
This project would allow me to work with image-based datasets, build data ingestion and processing pipelines, organize inspection results, and develop dashboards that show defect rates and production quality trends.
